Chapter 01 · Executive summary
The whole book in one page.

What we're building, in one page.

The product

JokesFor is the world's first joke search engine — a place where humor is treated as a canonical, taggable, save-able body of work. It's Spotify's recommendation craft applied to comedy, with Pinterest's collection ritual.

The opportunity

Jokes online today are scattered across Reddit threads, screenshot tweets, and joke-of-the-day spam apps. There is no clean, calm, well-lit place to find, save, and re-share humor. JokesFor is that place.

The user

The Toastmaster: someone who needs the right joke at the right moment — a parent, an MC, a manager opening a meeting, a developer breaking the ice in a code review. They don't want infinite scroll. They want the right joke, in seconds.

The design promise

Make the funny thing feel funny — without becoming a clown.

Calm white-space and editorial confidence on the outside; one well-placed lime accent and one italic wink doing all the comedic work on the inside. Restraint is the joke.

The four design moves

  1. Editorial layout, comedic accent. Newspaper-grade structure; one fluorescent punchline color.
  2. Format-aware joke cards. A one-liner does not look like a knock-knock. The card is part of the gag.
  3. A daily ritual. Streak, mystery box, 9 AM trigger. Hooked-loop design, not infinite-feed design.
  4. A serif-italic wink. One typographic gesture that says: this brand has a sense of humor about itself.

Chapter 02 · Strategic foundation

Two truths, one white space.

Every design decision in this book traces back to two strategic claims. If either fails, the design fails.

Truth 01

Humor is high-stakes.

A joke is a social bet. Tell it well: status. Tell it badly: shame. This is why people save jokes — they're saving social capital for later.

Design implication: jokes deserve curation, taxonomy, and previews. Never an infinite scroll of unfiltered noise. The product's job is to reduce risk.

Truth 02

Funny is identity.

The jokes you save say what kind of person you are: dad, dark, dry, absurd, wholesome. A joke library is a self-portrait.

Design implication: the library is a destination, not a dumping ground. Boards are personal. Vibe selection is identity-formation.

The white space — what nobody else does

REDDIT

Volume, no curation.

Endless threads. Great for discovery, terrible for retrieval. You can't find that joke again next Tuesday.

SPOTIFY (COMEDY)

Specials, not units.

Long-form audio. Brilliant recommendation engine, but for hour-long sets — not a single one-liner you can text a friend.

PINTEREST

Visual, not textual.

The board ritual we want. But comedy doesn't pin well as an image — it pins as language.

JokesFor occupies

A searchable, save-able, taggable library of unit-level jokes — with a daily ritual that turns retrieval into habit.

Chapter 04 · Color psychology

Why purple, why lime, why nothing else.

Color does the strategic work before the typography even arrives. The palette is small on purpose: one carrier color, one reward color, one warm accent — and a lot of disciplined whitespace.

Purple — creativity, play, the unexpected
#6A1CF6 · OKLCH 51% .27 295
Lime — the punchline, the reward
#CAFD00 · OKLCH 95% .25 122
Amber — warmth, mystery, surprise
#FFC965 · OKLCH 84% .15 80

The reasoning, color by color

Purple is the rarest hue in nature, which is exactly why brands use it for "imagination" — Twitch, Cadbury, Yahoo, Roku. It signals play without childishness. It's the right base for a product about wit.

Lime is fluorescent, jarring, and impossible to ignore — like a punchline. We use it for one thing only: the reward state. Save buttons, streak rails, the moment a punchline lands. Never as a body or chrome.

Amber is the warm storyteller — fireside, dad's armchair, the moment before the gag. It carries the Mystery Box, the Wholesome vibe, and the streak-saved confirmation.

The 60 / 30 / 10 / 3 rule

60% warm-white surface · 30% paper white cards · 10% purple chrome · 3% lime reward · amber appears only at story moments.

Accessibility

  • Purple #6A1CF6 on white passes WCAG AAA for body text (8.6:1).
  • Lime is never a foreground for body — only for short labels on its dark partner #3A4A00 (12.3:1).
  • Body text is always #1A1A1A on warm-white. No grey-on-grey.

Do / don't

DO

One lime moment per screen. The reward color loses meaning if it's everywhere.

DO

Lean on warm-whites. Pure white feels clinical. #FBFAF7 reads as paper.

DON'T

Never gradient-meme. No 5-color rainbow gradients. We are a serious place that loves jokes — not a children's app.

Chapter 06 · Voice & tone

How the brand talks.

Dry. Confident. Never explains the joke. Treats the user as another adult who already gets it.

Voice principles

  1. Don't laugh at your own jokes. The interface never says "lol" or "haha." That's the user's job.
  2. One sentence is the unit. Most copy in the product is one sentence. Often a fragment.
  3. A wink, not a wink-wink-nudge. The italic Fraunces moment in a headline is the only place we let the brand smirk.
  4. Plain over playful, when in doubt. Empty states are deadpan. The funny thing is the joke; the chrome is the straight man.

Chapter 07 · The Hooked loop

A daily ritual, engineered.

Nir Eyal's Hooked model has four phases: Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment. JokesFor's whole product is one disciplined loop.

01

Trigger

9 AM push: "Today's joke is ready." Internal trigger over time: morning coffee feels incomplete without it.

DAILY · 9AM
02

Action

One tap to open. One tap to save. Every action under 300ms. Friction is the enemy of habit.

2 TAPS · <300MS
03

Variable reward

You don't know which joke. Mystery box rolls. Punchline reveal. Dopamine lives in the gap, not the payoff.

UNPREDICTABLE
04

Investment

Save → board → vibe weights → tomorrow's joke is more "you." The streak number is the visible asset.

STREAK · 14D

Why this loop, specifically

JokesFor's product is built around one question per day, one answer per day. Not infinite scroll. The loop's quality matters more than its volume.

By making the variable reward both surprising (which joke?) and tuned (matched to your vibes), we get the dopamine of a slot machine with the satisfaction of a well-made gift.

Chapter 08 · UX laws applied

Four laws, four screens.

UX laws aren't decoration — each is the rationale for a specific screen choice.

HICK'S LAW

Time-to-decide grows with options.

Onboarding offers 12 vibes in a 3×4 grid — chunk-able by row, scannable in 4 seconds. Search filters are flat, not nested. The home grid shows 6 vibes, never 12.

SEEN ON · onboarding step 02 · home vibe rail
FITTS'S LAW

Big targets close to the cursor.

The Save button on JOTD is 44px tall and lives inside the hero card — within thumb / saccade distance of the punchline. Streak counter is right next to the JOTD title, not buried in profile.

SEEN ON · joke detail · home hero
ZEIGARNIK EFFECT

Open loops nag the brain.

The blurred punchline (chapter 9) creates a Zeigarnik gap: setup is delivered, payoff is suspended. The user can't not tap. The streak rail on home shows yesterday filled, today empty — same mechanism.

SEEN ON · joke formats · streak rail
GOAL-GRADIENT

Effort accelerates near a goal.

Streak progression shows "one more lands you in the Top 10%" at day 14 — proximity to a milestone is the reason day-15 conversion is 2× higher than day-7.

Chapter 09 · Joke-format taxonomy · INTERACTIVE

A one-liner shouldn't look like a knock-knock.

If the card is part of the gag, the format must be expressed in the layout. Six joke formats, six distinct cards, each with a tailored interaction. Tap any card.

01 · One-liner
No reveal
I told my wife she was drawing her eyebrows too high. She seemed surprised.

Design move: single line, set huge, balanced text wrap, the wink does the comedic work. No setup card, no reveal — the format is the punch.

02 · Setup & punchline
Tap to reveal
SETUP
Why don't scientists trust atoms anymore?
PUNCHLINE
Because they make up everything.

Design move: blurred punchline creates a Zeigarnik gap — the brain demands closure. Tap reveals; the gesture mirrors a comedian's pause. Anticipation is the reward.

03 · Knock-knock
Conversational
Knock, knock.
Who's there?
Lettuce.
Lettuce who?
Lettuce in, it's freezing out here.

Design move: chat-bubble dialogue, line-by-line reveal. The user is a participant. Each tap advances one line — staging the call-and-response that defines the format.

04 · Anti-joke
Deadpan
Why did the chicken cross the road?
— pause —
To get to the other side.

Design move: brutalist mono frame, no color, deliberate visible "pause" rule. The card itself is the gag — it refuses to perform. The absence of design is the design.

05 · Story / shaggy-dog
2-min read
STORY · 2 MIN · WHOLESOME

A man walks into a library and asks for a book on paranoia. The librarian whispers, "They're right behind you." He spins around — nobody. He turns back. The librarian is gone. The shelves are gone. The library is gone. He's standing in a field. A horse approaches and says, "Why the long face?"

Design move: serif body, drop-cap, paragraph rhythm. Reads like a tiny short story. Reading time eyebrow signals the commitment — long-form format demands long-form chrome.

06 · Observational
Quote-style
"Adulthood is just emailing 'Sounds good!' back and forth until one of you dies." — Observational · 4.8K saves

Design move: quote-style card with amber-rule and serif italic body. Treats the joke as commentary, not punchline. The format borrows authority from the "pull quote."

PSYCHOLOGY DIGEST

Zeigarnik effect

→ blurred punchline, dotted "pause" in anti-joke, advancing knock-knock

Variable reward

→ unknown punch behind the blur, unknown line behind the next "Knock"

Endowed progress

→ each tap on a knock-knock is investment; users finish what they start

Chapter 11 · Alternative direction
The Joke Times.
"All the jokes that fit, we print."Established 2026
Editorial · The case for newsprint
If JokesFor wanted to feel printed, here's what it would look like.

The recommended direction in this book — Curated Playful — keeps purple, lime, and the wink. It is the safest evolution of the existing draft, and the right call for a mainstream consumer launch.

This chapter is a second opinion. If JokesFor's positioning ever drifts older, weeklier, or more Substack-y, the brand has somewhere to go: a serif-led editorial system named, internally, The Joke Times.

It is the same product. The components are the same. The voice is the same. Only the visual register changes. We've held it in reserve as a future "Weekly Digest" sub-brand or a print-mode skin, and surface it here as a complete reference.
